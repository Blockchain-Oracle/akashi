title: Citation Verifier
description: How Akashi decides whether a citation is real, matches what it claims, and still stands. The service behind citation-verify.
---

Language models cite papers, cases and pages that do not exist, or real ones with the wrong year, authors or
title. The Citation Verifier looks each citation up in the records that define it and answers with a verdict, the
record it matched, and the fields that disagree.

It serves two endpoints under the capability ID `citation-verify`:

| Endpoint | What it answers |
|---|---|
| [`POST /v1/verify`](/docs/reference/cite/verify) | Up to 10 citations: does each exist, and does it match? |
| [`POST /v1/claim`](/docs/reference/cite/claim) | Does a source support a claim attributed to it? |

## How a check works

A request runs all its citations in parallel under one deadline of **8.5 seconds**. Each citation goes through
four stages.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Read the citation

The text is classified by the first thing it contains, in this order: a DOI, an arXiv ID, a PubMed ID, a US case
citation, a statute, a URL, and otherwise free text. You can also send a structured object with fields such as
`doi`, `title`, `authors` and `year` instead of a string.

</Step>
<Step>

### Look it up where it is defined

- **An identifier** (DOI, arXiv, PMID) is resolved directly: doi.org confirms the DOI is registered, and the record
  comes from its registration agency (Crossref, or DataCite for arXiv). A PubMed ID is looked up in PubMed and, when
  it carries a DOI, follows the DOI path.
- **Free text** is searched in Crossref. When no candidate matches cleanly, three more searches run in parallel:
  Crossref by title and first author, DataCite's arXiv records, and OpenAlex. Each candidate is scored on title,
  authors, year and venue, and only work-level records count (not journals, issues or correction notices).
- **A case citation** is checked against the Caselaw Access Project: does a case start on that page of that volume?
  See [Check a court case citation](/docs/services/cite/legal).
- **A URL** is fetched while the Wayback Machine is asked for an archived copy. A page that declares a
  `citation_doi` is verified as that DOI.

</Step>
<Step>

### Apply retractions

Crossref's update records (which include Retraction Watch) and OpenAlex's retraction flag are checked for every
matched work. A retraction overrides every other verdict. A correction or an expression of concern is reported in
`retraction.status` without changing the verdict.

</Step>
<Step>

### Answer, or say what could not be checked

Each citation gets one verdict. A source that did not answer in time is named in the envelope's `unavailable`
list, and the citations it affected come back `unverifiable` with `retryable: true`.

</Step>
</Steps>

## The verdicts at a glance

| Verdict | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `verified` | The work exists and the fields you gave match it | Use it |
| `mismatch` | The work exists, but a field is wrong; `field_diffs` shows which | Correct the citation |
| `not_found` | No record matches after a complete search | Treat the citation as invented |
| `retracted` | The work exists and has been retracted | Do not rely on it |
| `ambiguous` | Two records fit about equally well; `candidates` lists them | Add a DOI or more fields |
| `unverifiable` | It could not be checked: a source was down, or the input is outside coverage | Check `retryable` and `reasons` |

The full rules, with real answers, are in [Verify citations](/docs/services/cite/verdicts).

## Absence is claimed only after a complete search

A missing record proves something only if the search could have found it. For free-text citations, `not_found`
requires both Crossref and OpenAlex to have answered, because OpenAlex is the only index covering venues without
DOIs (JMLR, older NeurIPS). If either was missing, the answer is `unverifiable` with `retryable: true`. A search
miss also never claims more than 0.9 confidence: it is absence of evidence, not proof.

## What it does not check

- **Statutes** such as `42 U.S.C. § 1983` come back `unverifiable` with a pointer to Pocket's `us-code-cfr` service.
- **Case law past the Caselaw Access Project's coverage** is `unverifiable`: US Reports end in 2014 and F.3d in 2019.
- **Claims** are judged against the abstract or text you provide, not the full paper.
