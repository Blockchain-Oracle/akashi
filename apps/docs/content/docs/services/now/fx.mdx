---
title: Get an exchange rate
description: Get the rate between two currencies from the central banks that publish it, with each bank's fixing, its age in business days and whether the banks agree.
---

You will call `/v1/fx` for a currency pair and read which central bank each number came from. You need a way to call
Akashi (the [mppx CLI](/docs/calling/curl-and-mppx) is the quickest; see [Calling Akashi](/docs/calling)).

## When to use it

- **Converting a price or an invoice** where the rate must be an official one and its date must be stated.
- **Checking a rate a model quoted.** Rates move daily; the answer shows the fixing date of every source.
- **Spotting disagreement.** For some currencies the central banks that publish a rate differ by several percent;
  the answer says so instead of averaging.

Rates are central-bank reference fixings (published once a day, or once a week), not live market quotes.

## Request

<TypeTable
  type={{
    base: { type: "string", description: "ISO 4217 code of the currency you have, e.g. USD.", required: true },
    quotes: { type: "string[]", description: "ISO 4217 codes you want rates for; 1 to 10.", required: true },
    amount: { type: "number", description: "An amount of base to convert; the answer adds converted." },
    date: { type: "string (date)", description: "A historical date instead of the latest fixings." },
  }}
/>

## Send it

```json title="Request body"
{ "base": "USD", "quotes": ["EUR"] }
```

```bash title="Terminal"
MPPX_PRIVATE_KEY=0x... npx -y mppx@0.11.0 https://test.agent.pocket.network/v1/live-facts/v1/fx -J '{"base":"USD","quotes":["EUR"]}' --protocol x402
```

## Reading the answer

- **`rate`** is **0.88067**, the European Central Bank's fixing. When the quote currency's own central bank has a
  current fixing (`issuer: true`), its rate is the headline; otherwise the headline is the median of the current
  fixings.
- **`providers`** lists every bank consulted, up to four: the currency's issuers first, then ECB, FRED, BOC, BOE, RBA
  and BOJ, then any other bank that publishes the pair. Rates come through Frankfurter, which serves each bank's own
  fixing.
- **`date`** and **`business_days_old`** give each fixing's age. Banks publish at different hours, so fixings within
  one business day of the newest are compared as current; that is why BOC and ECB (29 September) and BOE
  (28 September) all count.
- **FRED is `lagging`**: its H.10 series is weekly, so a fixing from 25 September is on schedule but older than the
  rest. It is listed and left out of the comparison. A fixing becomes `stale` only when it is older than its bank's
  cadence allows (2 business days for a daily bank, 6 for a weekly one).
- **`agreement: agree`** with **`spread_pct: 0.2566`**: the current fixings are within 0.5 % of each other. Up to
  1.5 % is `minor_diff`; beyond that, `conflict`.

## Edge cases

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="The banks disagree">
    USD→NGN is published by the Central Bank of Nigeria and by the central banks of Tanzania, Angola and The Gambia.
    On 30 September 2026 The Gambia's fixing was about 9 % below the others: the answer kept the Central Bank of
    Nigeria's rate as the headline, listed every fixing, and reported `agreement: conflict` with `spread_pct: 9.55`.
    See the worked example in [How Live Facts works](/docs/services/now).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which banks count as independent">
    Only banks that publish their own daily or weekly fixing are compared. Euro-area national banks republish the
    ECB's fixing to the digit, so only the ECB counts for them; aggregators with no country (IMF, BIS) and monthly
    averages are left out.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="No central bank publishes the pair">
    The call fails with `422 invalid_input`, naming the pair.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Frankfurter is down">
    The answer falls back to the ECB's own daily XML file and computes cross rates from it. The envelope is `partial`,
    `unavailable` names `frankfurter`, and each result has one provider, `ECB` ("European Central Bank (direct)").
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How fresh is a repeated call">
    Rates are cached for 10 minutes and the list of banks for 24 hours. Fixings change at most once a day, so this does
    not hide a newer rate for long.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
